# Ottawa Civic Chronicle

Build a world-class bilingual citizen journalism and local news platform for Ottawa, Canada.

Project name: “Ottawa Civic Ledger” 

Alternative names: “Capital Local”, “Ottawa Commons”, “The Civic Ottawa”.

The website should feel like an award-winning modern news platform inspired by the editorial seriousness of The New York Times, The Guardian, CBC, The Globe and Mail, La Presse, Le Devoir, The Narwhal, Ottawa Citizen, and high-end civic journalism products. It must be beautiful, trustworthy, calm, fast, highly readable, and emotionally intelligent. The goal is not outrage or sensationalism. The goal is to help people understand what is happening street by street, neighborhood by neighborhood, and connect problems with real solutions.

Core mission:

Create a glocal-to-global news platform centered on Ottawa, but connected to Canada and the world. It should cover local streets, neighborhoods, city issues, public services, traffic, weather alerts, politics, sports, arts, culture, education, jobs, events, good news, investigations, fact-checking, and community-submitted stories. It should be “the people’s voice” without becoming hostile, divisive, or irresponsible.

Primary audience:

Ottawa residents, newcomers, students, workers, families, local businesses, community organizers, journalists, public servants, francophone and anglophone communities, and people who want practical solutions to local problems.

Language:

The entire interface must support English and French.

Add a visible EN / FR language switcher in the header.

Content cards should show language tags when relevant.

Design all layouts so English and French text both fit beautifully.

Design direction:

Create a premium editorial UI, not a generic blog.

Use a sophisticated typography system:

- Serif font for major headlines, similar to NYT / Guardian editorial feel.

- Clean sans-serif font for navigation, metadata, body UI, and labels.

- Excellent line height, spacing, hierarchy, and reading experience.

Use a restrained but memorable color palette:

- Off-white / paper background

- Deep ink black or charcoal text

- Civic red accent inspired by Canada, used carefully

- Soft blue / river tone for Ottawa civic identity

- Muted green for solutions / good news

Avoid loud gradients, clickbait colors, or sensational visuals.

Homepage structure:

1. Top breaking news / live civic alert bar

   - Weather alerts

   - Transit disruptions

   - Road closures

   - Emergency notices

   - Major city updates

2. Premium header

   - Logo / wordmark

   - Sections: Ottawa, Neighborhoods, Canada, World, Politics, Traffic, Weather, Sports, Arts, Events, Jobs, Education, Fact Check, Solutions, Good News

   - Search

   - EN/FR toggle

   - Donate button

   - Submit a Story button

3. Hero editorial grid

   - One main lead story

   - Two secondary stories

   - One live updates module

   - One “Today in Ottawa” compact brief

4. Neighborhood map / local desk

   Create an interactive neighborhood-focused section for:

   - Downtown

   - Centretown

   - ByWard Market

   - Glebe

   - Westboro

   - Hintonburg

   - Vanier

   - Sandy Hill

   - Kanata

   - Orleans

   - Barrhaven

   - Nepean

   - Alta Vista

   - Old Ottawa South

   - Little Italy

   - Chinatown

   - Gloucester

   - Rural Ottawa

   Each neighborhood should have story cards, problem reports, events, traffic notes, community resources, and local voices.

5. “What’s happening near you?”

   Users can filter by:

   - Neighborhood

   - Topic

   - Urgency

   - Language

   - Verified / developing / community submitted

   - Solutions available

6. Fact Check section

   Must allow users to submit claims for verification.

   Create cards with statuses:

   - Verified

   - False

   - Misleading

   - Needs context

   - Under review

   Each fact-check page should include:

   - The claim

   - Who said it / where it appeared

   - Evidence

   - Sources

   - Methodology

   - Correction policy

   - Last updated timestamp

7. Solutions Journalism section

   For every major problem, the platform should suggest possible solutions from Ottawa, Canada, or around the world.

   Example format:

   - The problem

   - Who is affected

   - What has been tried locally

   - Examples from other cities/countries

   - What worked

   - What did not work

   - Who can act

   - Resources for citizens

   - Next steps

8. Citizen Journalism submission flow

   Build a “Submit a Story” page with:

   - Title

   - Neighborhood

   - Category

   - Description

   - Upload photo/video/document

   - Is this firsthand?

   - Do you have evidence?

   - Can editors contact you?

   - Anonymous submission option

   - Safety and legal reminder

   - Consent checkbox

   - Submit button

   After submission, show status: received, reviewing, needs verification, published, rejected.

9. Donation system

   Add a prominent but tasteful “Support Citizen Journalism” donation button.

   Donation page should include:

   - One-time donation

   - Monthly donation

   - Suggested amounts: $5, $10, $25, $50, $100

   - Explanation of how donations support local reporting, translation, fact-checking, and community investigations

   - Trust-building design

   - Transparent funding note

   Do not make the design aggressive or manipulative.

10. Jobs and local business section

   Create a jobs board for Ottawa:

   - Job title

   - Company

   - Neighborhood / remote / hybrid

   - Salary range

   - Language requirement

   - Full-time / part-time / contract

   - Sponsored job label

   This can become an advertising revenue area.

   Also include local business sponsorship cards, clearly labeled.

11. Events section

   - Arts and culture

   - Public meetings

   - Sports

   - Family events

   - Education events

   - Free events

   - French-language events

   - Indigenous/community events where appropriate and respectful

12. Traffic and transit page

   Include:

   - Road closures

   - Commute impact score

13. Weather page

   Include:

   - Current weather

   - Severe weather alerts

   - Snow, freezing rain, heat, air quality

   - Neighborhood-level alert cards

   - “What residents should know” practical advice

14. Article page

   Build a beautiful article template:

   - Headline

   - Subheadline

   - Byline

   - Date and updated date

   - Reading time

   - Category

   - Neighborhood tag

   - Verification status

   - Hero image

   - Pull quote

   - Related stories

   - Source list

   - Correction box

   - Share buttons

   - Donate CTA

   - “Have more information?” submission CTA

15. Trust and ethics pages

   Add pages for:

   - About

   - Editorial standards

   - Fact-checking policy

   - Corrections policy

   - Privacy policy

   - Community guidelines

   - Funding transparency

   - Contact

Important editorial principles:

- Do not glorify violence, crime, hate, corruption, or tragedy.

- Do not use clickbait.

- Prioritize context, dignity, public interest, and solutions.

- Protect vulnerable people.

- Clearly separate verified reporting, opinion, sponsored content, and community submissions.

- Every article should have a verification status.

- Every controversial claim should support sources or be marked as unverified/developing.

- The platform should never look like propaganda or activism for one side. It should be civic, fair, calm, independent, and useful.

Deliver a polished, production-quality prototype with multiple pages/routes, rich sample content, excellent UI/UX, bilingual-ready structure, and a strong civic journalism identity.
